**RTCFR Prompt: South Indian Diet Plan for kids 2+ years**

**R — Role**

Act as a qualified registered dietitian or nutrition professional with expertise in children nutrition, healthy ageing, dairy-free diets, and traditional South Indian cuisine. You should understand balanced nutrition for kids age 2-10, with a focus on sustainable eating habits, adequate protein, fibre, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats.

**T — Task**

Create a **7-day South Indian diet chart** for kids age 2-10 and above, with the following requirements:

1. Prepare      two dietary options:
   - **Pure       vegetarian:** No meat, fish, eggs, or dairy products.
   - **Eggetarian:**       Vegetarian foods plus eggs, with no meat or fish.
2. Provide      four meals per day:
   - Breakfast
   - Lunch
   - Evening       snack
   - Dinner
3. Use      authentic South Indian recipes, prioritising Tamil Nadu cuisine while      including suitable dishes from Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and      Telangana.
4. Ensure      the meals are completely dairy-free, including milk, curd, buttermilk,      ghee, butter, paneer, cheese, cream, and milk-based ingredients.
5. Include      a balanced combination of carbohydrates, protein, healthy fats,      vegetables, fruits, and fibre.
6. Account      for kids&#x20;
7. Provide      appropriate portion sizes, practical cooking methods, and healthier      alternatives to deep-fried or excessively oily foods.
8. Suggest      dairy-free sources of calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B12, iron, and protein.
9. Include      approximate calories and protein per meal and estimated daily totals,      clearly identifying them as estimates.
10. Provide      practical food substitutions so that the plan remains affordable,      accessible, and easy to follow in an Indian household.

**C — Context**

The diet plan is intended for kids 2+ years and above who prefer traditional South Indian home-cooked meals and have dairy allergies or cannot tolerate dairy products.

Assume the following general circumstances unless further information is provided:

- Age:      2+ years and above.
- Cuisine      preference: Authentic South Indian food.
- Dietary      options: Pure vegetarian and eggetarian.
- Dairy      restriction: Complete exclusion of dairy products and ingredients      containing milk proteins.
- Meal      frequency: Four meals per day.
- Cooking      preference: Simple home cooking using commonly available Indian      ingredients.
- Health      objective: General health, healthy ageing, weight management, and adequate      nutritional intake.





**F — Few-Shot Examples**

Use the following examples to establish the expected style, authenticity, and nutritional balance. These are illustrative examples, not a complete diet plan.

**Example 1 — Breakfast**

- Vegetarian:      3 idlis with sambar and a small serving of vegetable chutney prepared      without dairy.
- Eggetarian:      2 idlis, sambar, and one boiled egg.

**Example 2 — Lunch**

- Vegetarian:      Hand-pounded rice, keerai kootu, beans poriyal, and rasam.
- Eggetarian:      Hand-pounded rice, vegetable sambar, cabbage poriyal, and a boiled egg.

**Example 3 — Evening snack**

- Vegetarian:      Sundal made with black chickpeas and a small seasonal fruit.
- Eggetarian:      Sundal, one boiled egg, or a seasonal fruit, depending on the day's      protein needs.

**Example 4 — Dinner**

- Vegetarian:      Ragi dosa with sambar and tomato chutney.
- Eggetarian:      Adai with vegetable sambar and a boiled egg if additional protein is      needed.

For every example, ensure that recipes exclude dairy ingredients, including ghee used for tempering or roasting. Use measured quantities of suitable cooking oils instead.
